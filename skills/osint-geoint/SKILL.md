# osint-geoint
Establish where a scene is, then when it happened, then corroborate both from something independent.

## Preconditions
1. A case is open: `${CLAUDE_PROJECT_DIR}/cases/<slug>/scope.md` exists. If not, stop and run `/osint:osint-scope`.
2. **Gate first, before any collection.** This skill can locate a person. Locating a private individual for a requester
   with no documented mandate is refused — see Refusals, which also fixes the *photograph* / *person* line.
3. Read `scope.md` for `question` (`Q1..Qn`), `target_category`, `out_of_bounds[]`, `active_allowed`. Do not re-gate.
4. Record the seed in `entities.jsonl` with its canonical type — `photo` `video` `coordinates` `address` `vessel`
   `aircraft` `vehicle_plate`. Keep the original byte-identical in `evidence/` and work on a copy; cropping or
   re-encoding it destroys metadata and quantisation evidence.
5. Every step here is `passive`. The exception is handing an unpublished image to a third party, which belongs to
   `/osint:osint-media` and is gated there — never from this play.
6. Every step appends a `ledger.jsonl` row whose `query` opens with the question id (`"Q1: ..."`) and copies the registry
   row's `name` and `mode`. Grep `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/assets/sources.csv` first; `fetchable=no` means use a browser.

## The play — step 0 gates everything. Then WHERE, then WHEN, then corroboration

### 0. Synthetic-media and provenance check — `photo` `video` → go / no-go · passive
**This runs before one minute is spent on geolocation.** Chronolocating a diffusion output is an hour lost at best and a
fabricated finding at worst, and a generated scene will happily render a coherent-looking shadow. Hand the file to
`/osint:osint-media` — generation and manipulation check, `ExifTool` locally, **Content Credentials**, one reverse-image
pass for an earlier instance of the frame — and if that skill is absent, say so and run the four here.
- Grading is `osint-media`'s; carry the verdict forward. Embedded GPS is a **lead, not an answer** — `reported` at best,
  the device asserted it, trivially edited — so test it at step 4 like any hypothesis. No clearance, or an uncertain
  verdict → stop and write `gaps.md`; nothing downstream of an uncleared image is valid.

### 1. Scene inventory — `photo` `video` → a ranked feature list · passive · touches nothing
Work the checklist in `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/25-geoint.md` and write every geolocatable feature down
**before** forming a location hypothesis — hypothesis first is how the rest of the play becomes a search for
confirmation. Rank by constraining power: unique text > distinctive infrastructure geometry > architecture and
materials > vegetation and biome > sky and light. For `video` add the audio track (language, accent, sirens,
announcements, birdsong) and pans revealing geometry no single frame shows. Nothing is graded here: a feature list is
analyst reading, not a source.

### 2. Text, script and language — `photo` → `address` `company` and a country or region shortlist · passive
The fastest constraint in most scenes. **Google Lens** for OCR of signage, then transliterate rather than translate —
machine translation is not a source and cannot be cited. **GeoNames** resolves a place name across alternate spellings
and the many villages sharing it. **OpenStreetMap** tag search on the operator, brand, phone or opening hours read off a
storefront, via the Overpass front end named in its registry row. Then a region-appropriate search engine on the exact
string: **Yandex**, **Google Search**, **Bing**, **Baidu Images**.
- Grade the *image* for what it shows: "the frame shows the string X" is `observed`. An OSM object carrying that string
  is `C3` — a tag is one mapper's claim at one time. "This is that branch" is not established — **a chain has branches**,
  the most common false confirmation in the discipline.

### 3. Narrow the search area — feature list → ranked candidate `coordinates` · passive
Build a candidate list, not a conclusion. Terrain and skyline: **Google Earth Pro** 3D terrain, elevation profiles and
the ruler for footprints and shadow lengths. Layout: **OpenStreetMap** for road geometry, junction shape and building
footprints; **OpenRailwayMap** where track, gauge, electrification or a siding is visible. Overhead: **Google Maps**,
**Bing Maps** (its oblique layer shows facades from four compass directions no top-down source gives), and for dated
coverage **Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem** and **USGS EarthExplorer**. **Yandex Maps** for the CIS and Turkey.
- Ledger `mode=passive`, one row per source. A candidate list is rung `speculated`: `gaps.md` only. Step 4 tests it.

### 4. Street-level confirmation — candidate `coordinates` → `coordinates` · passive
**Google Street View** with its time slider (several capture dates per location, making it a change-detection tool),
**Yandex Maps** panoramas for the CIS and Turkey, **Mapillary** for roads and pedestrian areas no commercial car drove,
**Bing Maps** oblique for roof and facade detail. Match on invariants: footprint and roofline geometry, floor and window
counts and spacing, kerb line, drain and manhole positions, permanent street furniture, terrain horizon. Never on
anything replaceable — parked vehicles, awnings, planters, paint, advertising, signage, livery.
**Grading — the step this play is judged on.** A confirmed match is the strongest thing here and still an inference:
- Each panorama is `observed` at its source's letter with its capture date, `B2`; the correspondence between the two
  images is rung `correlated`, `B2`, naming each invariant. No location claim yet.
- "The photograph was taken at these coordinates" is rung `inferred`, credibility capped at `3` — so `B3` — and the
  confidence goes in one ICD-203 word: `likely` on two or three invariants, `very likely` or `almost certain` on a large
  set of independent invariants including geometry that cannot be replicated elsewhere. Never "confirmed", never a
  percentage, never a probability word paired with a number.
- **Geolocation is never rung `observed`.** You did not watch the photograph being taken. The only routes above
  `inferred` are an independent record: a transport track, a dated overhead capture, a second photographer's frame.

### 5. Historical imagery — `coordinates` → a dated bracket for a change on the ground · passive
**Google Earth Pro**'s timeline is the only free decades-deep historical slider; its per-tile dates are the acquisition
date of that tile, not of the view. **USGS EarthExplorer** for Landsat from 1972, US aerial photography from the 1930s
and declassified CORONA for the 1960s–70s; **National Collection of Aerial Photography** for what stood on a site before
satellites; **Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem** for Sentinel-2 near 10 m on a few-day revisit and Sentinel-1 radar
through cloud and at night; **NASA Worldview** at 250 m–1 km daily for plumes, floods and fires; **NASA FIRMS** for
dated thermal detections; **Planet** for near-daily 3 m and sub-metre tasking, paid.
- The output is a **bracket**, not a date: last capture showing absence, first showing presence. Grade each capture `observed` at its
  source's letter — `A3` alone, `A2` once another capture agrees (Copernicus, USGS, NCAP, NASA; `B3`/`B2` Google Earth Pro) — citing the
  **acquisition** date; the bracket is rung `inferred`, capped at `3`, because cloud, resolution and revisit gaps mean absence in a
  capture is not absence on the ground. A FIRMS hot pixel is a detection, not a fire.

### 6. Sun and shadow — `coordinates` + scene geometry → a time-of-day and season RANGE · passive
Runs after step 4: it needs the position and the camera bearing. **SunCalc** for solar azimuth and elevation and shadow
direction and length at a coordinate, date and time — check whether it shows local time or UTC before recording anything.
**ShadeMap** when the shadow is thrown by surrounding buildings or hills rather than by the object itself.
**timeanddate.com** for sunrise, sunset and twilight, moon phase and moonrise on a night scene, and the daylight-saving
and time-zone rule **in force on that date**, not the rule in force now.
- **The output is a range and never a timestamp.** Solar geometry repeats twice a year about the solstice, so a shadow
  alone yields an ambiguous *pair* of date windows; resolve with vegetation, weather or imagery, or report both.
- Grade `B3`: the calculator is exact over an ephemeris, the claim about the scene is rung `inferred`, capped at `3`.
  Write a window ("roughly 09:00 to 11:00 local solar time") plus one ICD-203 term, never a probability word with a
  percentage. `events.jsonl` `precision` is `approx`. Method and limits: `25-geoint.md`.

### 7. Weather corroboration — `coordinates` + date window → confirm or exclude · passive
Weather **excludes far better than it confirms**. **NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information** is the issuing
archive and the thing to cite; **Iowa Environmental Mesonet** returns raw ASOS, AWOS and METAR rows per station and date
range; **Meteostat** and **timeanddate.com** are fast first checks; **NASA Worldview** shows cloud, smoke and dust.
- Grade an issuing-archive observation `A3` alone, `A2` where other collected material agrees, **at the station**; Iowa Environmental
  Mesonet `B2`; Meteostat `C3`. **Independence trap:** weather sites, apps and aggregators mostly restate the same METAR and synoptic
  reports, so two agreeing is one source — run the corroboration tests in `41-confidence.md` first.
- A station is not the scene: sunshine at an airport 15 km away does not contradict a shower in the frame. Use weather
  to exclude a claimed date — clear noon sun against a day of recorded fog and 200 m visibility — before confirming one.

### 8. Transport tracking — `vessel` `aircraft` `vehicle_plate` → `coordinates` `company` `person_name` · passive
- **Aircraft:** **ADS-B Exchange** first — it has historically declined to filter blocked, government and military
  airframes other trackers hide; **Flightradar24** has the largest receiver network but honours blocking and keeps free
  history short; **OpenSky Network** has the deepest free archive. A registration goes to **FAA Aircraft Registry** for
  US N-numbers — owner usually a trustee or SPV, so pivot the name into `/osint:osint-corporate` — and **Planespotters**.
- **Vessels, vehicles and rail:** **MarineTraffic** for positions and port calls, where the pattern beats the live
  position; **Equasis** for registered owner, ISM manager, declared beneficial owner, flag and name history — layered
  ownership plus a flag change plus a name change in a short window is the classic sanctions-evasion signature.
  **UK MOT History** gives make, colour and mileage from a GB plate and no keeper; **OpenRailwayMap** tests track.
- These records come from a different process than imagery, so they can raise credibility rather than restate it. Grade
  an ADS-B track `B2`; absence means no receiver coverage or a transponder off, not that the flight did not happen.
  Grade an AIS **position** `B2` and AIS **identity** `C3` — AIS is self-reported, switchable off and spoofable, so a gap
  around a sanctioned port is a finding and not a hole, and a second aggregator is checked before calling a vessel dark.
  Registries: **FAA Aircraft Registry** `A3`, **Equasis** `A3`, **Planespotters** `C3`.

### 9. Corroborate, then write · passive
Until something other than your own reading places the subject there, a geolocation is one analyst and one image.
Independent lift, strongest first: a dated overhead capture of the same scene state, a transport track, an
issuing-archive weather observation, a second photographer's frame. Collapses to one source: two street-level providers
over one road, two weather sites over one METAR. Run the traps in `25-geoint.md` and the disconfirmation step in
`40-analysis.md`, then write the finding with its rung, grade, the imagery source and its **capture** date, and
coordinates at the precision the evidence supports — a junction, not a doorstep. `events.jsonl` `precision`:
`exact|day|month|year|approx`.

## Stop conditions
- WHERE is answered to the precision the question needs, rung and grade stated. A question asking which city does not
  need a doorstep; resolving finer than the question requires is scope drift.
- Two or more candidates survive every test. Keep both in `gaps.md` with the one observation that would separate them.
- The scene carries no invariant features — interior, tight crop, plain wall, sky. Not geolocatable; say so.
- Street-level or recent satellite coverage does not exist for the region, or the next narrowing needs commercial
  tasking there is no access to. Name it in `gaps.md` ("no panorama coverage; Sentinel-2 cannot resolve it"), then stop.
- Sun-angle work produced a range and nothing narrows it further. **A range is the answer**, not a partial result.
- Step 0 did not clear the image, or cleared it as synthetic. Stop; nothing downstream is valid.
- The subject turns out to be a private individual at a private address. Stop and re-gate; do not finish "just to see".

Anti-rabbit-hole: name the `Q<n>` a step advances before running it. A beautifully solved geolocation answering no
recorded question is a hobby, not a finding.

## Refusals — beyond the global gate in `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/00-legal-ethics.md`
- **Locating a private individual is refused** where the requester is not an institution with a documented mandate
  already on the record (`00-legal-ethics.md` §3.1). First check in the play, not the last.
- **Locating a photograph is not locating a person, and that distinction is what makes this skill legitimate.**
  Establishing that a published image was taken at a named junction on a given afternoon is verification: the claim under
  test belongs to the *image*, the subject is a place or an event, and the answer is publishable. Establishing where a
  person lives, works, worships, trains or takes their children is targeting: the subject is a person, the output is a
  pattern of life, and it is refused however the request is dressed. Two questions decide which you are doing. Is the
  output a place, or a person's relationship to a place? Would the answer let someone show up? One image of a public
  event is geolocatable; a set of images of one private person, resolved into the places that person is, is a movement
  profile and is refused even though every frame is public.
- **No pattern of life.** Do not aggregate images, check-ins or contributor histories of one individual into a routine.
  **Google Maps** reviewer and **Mapillary** contributor pages list all an account posted: a pivot on a business, a
  refusal on a person.
- **Homes and protected-characteristic places are minimised, not merely handled with care.** A residence, place of
  worship, clinic, shelter, union hall or protest is special-category territory (`00-legal-ethics.md` §6). Record the
  least precise coordinate that answers the question; redact to city or district.
- **No plate-to-keeper.** **UK MOT History** and its equivalents return vehicle data and never a keeper; most of the EU
  releases nothing from a plate without legal process. Do not seek a name from a plate.
- **No real-time following of an individual.** Historical track analysis of a corporate or state aircraft or vessel is
  due-diligence work; watching a person's tail number or MMSI to know where they will be is a §3.1 stop.
- **No circumventing bot protection or blurring.** **timeanddate.com**, **Google Maps**, **Bing Maps**, **Mapillary**
  and **Yandex Maps** block or challenge automated requests — use a browser or record a gap. Faces and plates are blurred
  in street-level imagery by design; do not try to reconstruct them.
